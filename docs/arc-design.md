# The arc — "The Listener"

Story architecture across all case packs. Design only; Pack 1's hook is the only
part written into content so far.

Written end-first, per the storytelling skill: the last reveal is fixed below,
and everything earlier exists to earn it.

---

## The lens

The obvious version of this story is *a brilliant serial killer taunts a great
detective*. That has been made a hundred times, and worse, it fights the medium:
this game is made of **read messages**, and a taunting villain would have to
send some.

So the villain is inverted twice.

**He does not kill.** Every murder in every pack has a real, ordinary, guilty
killer who did it for their own reasons — money, fear, a son. He is never in the
room. There is no forensic trace of him because there is nothing of his to trace.

**He never writes.** He only ever telephones. In a game where the player reads
everyone's messages, he is the one person whose words are never in the
transcript. You can never read him. You can only ever read the **effect** of him:
the moment somebody's behaviour changes, right after a ninety-second call.

That is why he is uncatchable in-world, and it is why he is frightening in *this*
game specifically.

**What he actually does:** he finds people already standing at the edge of it,
and he listens. He never suggests. He never instructs. He waits until they say
the thing out loud themselves — and then he agrees with them. There is no
sentence of his that any prosecutor could use, because he has never said one.

The detectives who have noticed him at all file him as **the Listener**.

## The detective, and why they are challenged

The player is genuinely excellent and the game should never undercut that. They
solve every case. Provably, by the rules, every time.

The challenge is structural rather than a battle of wits: **arrangement leaves no
text.** So every pack ends the same way — the player wins completely and loses
completely. They prove exactly who did it. They cannot prove who arranged it.

That tension is the engine of the whole campaign, and it costs nothing to
maintain, because each case is genuinely solved.

## The ending, written first

The final pack is a murder the player cannot solve, because it is not one. It is
a suicide the Listener talked someone into.

Breaking it requires the player to accept the thing the whole campaign has been
walking them toward: **their own most famous closed case — the one their
reputation is built on — was his first.** The career was built on his work.

Everything before this exists to make that land rather than merely surprise.

## Structure — 9 packs

| Pack | Role | Listener content |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | The hook | A call that should not exist, and a lie that can be proven |
| 2 | Standalone | none |
| **3** | Connection | Clue 2, in the confession only |
| 4, 5 | Standalone | none |
| **6** | Connection | Clue 3, in the confession only |
| 7, 8 | Standalone | none |
| **9** | The uncovering | He is named |

Packs 2, 4, 5, 7, 8 are **genuinely unconnected**. This is load-bearing: if every
pack connects, the player stops reading cases and starts scanning for the arc.
The silence is what makes packs 3 and 6 land.

**The reveal rule:** a connection surfaces *only* in the confrontation, after the
case is already solved. Never in the briefing, never in the threads. The player
must have earned the whole case before the floor moves.

## Clue economy

What the player accumulates about him. Each is a fact, not an atmosphere.

| # | Pack | The clue |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 1 | He telephoned. He never texted. Around ninety seconds. And what he said was **provably false**. |
| 2 | 3 | He knew something private — something only someone with access could know. He had been close to the victim's life before the killer ever was. |
| 3 | 6 | He has been doing this for a very long time, and he selects people already at the edge. The choosing is the crime. |
| 4 | 9 | The first one. |

**Fair play:** every clue is a thing the player can point at in the record, not an
inference the game makes for them. Clue 1 is a *contradiction* — the same
mechanic as everything else. He said the papers were sent; the papers were at the
scene. The engine can already prove it.

## Red herrings — "it could be anyone"

Once a clue is known, later packs seed suspects who match it **innocently**.

- After clue 1 (*never writes*): a suspect who never texts. Because they cannot
  read well, and are ashamed of it.
- After clue 2 (*had access*): a suspect who knew the victim's private business,
  because they cleaned the house.
- After clue 3 (*long time*): someone old enough, in the right places, at the
  right times — and simply unlucky.

**Rule:** a red herring must be innocent for a reason the player can also prove.
A suspect who merely *looks* guilty and is then dropped is a cheat. One who looks
guilty, and whose innocence the player can establish with the same tools, is the
game keeping its promise.

## Keeping packs different

Each pack changes the **premise**, not just the cast: a lighthouse, a night
ferry, a research station, a wedding, a hospital ward, a film set. New place, new
cast, new shape of lie. The Listener is the only recurring element, and he
appears in three of nine.

## Length — the honest version

Case 1 is 76 messages and runs about 20 minutes.

**15 hours per pack is not reachable.** It would be roughly 3,400 messages and
~50,000 words per pack — a short novel each, nine times over — and one mechanic
sustained for fifteen unbroken hours would exhaust itself regardless of how good
the writing was.

**The target is 15 hours across the campaign.**

| | |
|---|---|
| Pack | 2 linked cases, ~45 min each ≈ **90 minutes** |
| Campaign | 9 packs ≈ **13.5 hours** |
| Per pack | ~350 messages, ~5 characters per case, 3 contradictions + 1 motive |

That is a real 15-hour game, built one shippable pack at a time, and each pack
stays inside the length where this mechanic is still a pleasure.

**For the hackathon deadline (2026-09-30):** Pack 1 only, plus the hook. Pack 2
onward is post-submission.

## Pack 1 — the hook

Case 1, "The Lighthouse", is already written and already solvable. The hook is a
**retrofit to the confession**, not a rewrite: nothing about Mairi's guilt
changes.

Her existing confession already ends:

> *"I did not decide anything. I have gone over it every hour since and I cannot
> find the moment I decided."*

That line was written before any of this, and it is the door. She cannot find the
moment she decided **because it was not hers.**

**The added beat:** someone telephoned the café that evening. A man. He said he
was calling from the Trust's auditors, and that Ruth had already sent the papers
— that by morning it would be out of her hands and Callum's name would be in them
permanently.

**It was a lie, and the player can prove it.** Ruth never sent anything. She had
written it all in the back of the survey notebook, in her own hand, and the
notebook was still at the scene — the epilogue already establishes this, and Esme
already mentions the notebook in the group thread on day two.

So the first clue about the Listener is a contradiction between a statement and a
recovered fact. Same rules, same board, same proof. No new mechanic.

**The signature, in Mairi's words:** he never once mentioned hurting her. He
barely spoke at all. She talked, and he listened, and when she said the thing out
loud herself he said only:

> *"Then you already know."*

**What this does to Case 1:** Mairi is still guilty. She still chose. But she was
chosen first — and the case gets a second, colder ending underneath the one it
already has, without weakening the first.
