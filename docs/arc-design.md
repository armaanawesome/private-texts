# The arc — "The Listener"

Story architecture across all 15 case packs. Design only; Pack 1's hook is the
only part written into content so far.

Written end-first, per the storytelling skill: the ending below is fixed, and
everything earlier exists to earn it.

**Process for every pack** (standing instruction, 2026-08-11):

| Layer | Skill |
|---|---|
| Case + arc structure | `storytelling` |
| The actual in-game messages | `anti-ai-writing` |
| Blurbs, pack openers, the arc hook | `viral-hooks` |

---

## The lens

The obvious version is *a brilliant serial killer taunts a great detective*.
Made a hundred times, and worse, it fights the medium: this game is **read
messages**, and a taunting villain has to send some.

So he is inverted twice.

**He does not kill.** Every murder in every pack has a real, ordinary, guilty
killer with their own reasons — money, fear, a son. He is never in the room.
There is no forensic trace because there is nothing of his to trace.

**He does not write — to anyone in a case.** To the people he works on, he only
ever telephones. Around ninety seconds. Which is why no case file anywhere
contains a single word of his.

What he does: he finds people already standing at the edge, and he listens. He
never suggests. Never instructs. He waits until they say it out loud themselves,
then agrees. No prosecutor can quote a sentence, because he has never said one.

They call him **the Listener**.

## His voice — the one exception

He writes to exactly one person: **you**.

This is the deliberate crack in his own discipline, and it has to be in
character rather than a plot convenience. Everyone else is material. You are the
only one who might understand the work. He is not careless — he is *choosing* to
be read, by an audience of one.

**One thread, pinned at the top of the inbox from Pack 1 to Pack 15.** A new
number every time; the old ones go dead.

**The rule that keeps him frightening:** he never threatens, never gloats, never
uses the word kill. He reviews your work. He is a critic writing to an artist —
he congratulates you on Ardnoe, notes where you were slow, says he expected
better on the ferry business. Praise from him is worse than a threat, and none of
it is prosecutable, which is exactly why he permits himself to send it.

**The mechanic:** for fourteen packs the reply field on that thread is dead. You
can read him. You cannot answer.

In Pack 15, you can.

## The detective

You are genuinely excellent and the game never undercuts it. You solve every
case, provably, by the rules.

The challenge is structural: **arrangement leaves no text.** So every pack ends
the same way — you win completely and lose completely. You prove exactly who did
it. You cannot prove who arranged it.

## The ending, written first

**You catch him.** The final pack's confrontation is against the Listener
himself, which is the payoff the whole game has been training: fifteen packs
teaching you to break a story with proven contradictions, against the one man who
has never left one.

So how do you break a man who never asserts anything?

**His single lie.** In Pack 1 he told Mairi Bell that Ruth had already sent the
papers. That is the only time in fifteen packs he ever *stated a fact* instead of
listening — a false statement made to procure a death. You recorded it in the
tutorial case without knowing what you had.

The first clue in the game is the thing that hangs him.

**His confession:** your first famous case, the one your whole reputation rests
on, was his. He arranged it, watched you solve it, and has been following you
ever since. He did not choose you because you were the best. You are the best
because he chose you.

## Structure — 15 packs

| Packs | Tier | Listener content |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | free | **The hook.** A call that should not exist, and a lie you can prove |
| 2 | free | none |
| **3** | free | **Connection 1** — clue 2, in the confession only |
| 4, 5 | paid | none |
| **6** | paid | **Connection 2** — clue 3 |
| 7, 8 | paid | none |
| **9** | paid | **Connection 3** — clue 4 |
| 10, 11 | paid | none |
| **12** | paid | **Connection 4** — clue 5, the one that narrows him to a person |
| 13, 14 | paid | none |
| **15** | paid | **You catch him** |

**The free tier ends on a reveal.** Packs 1–3 are free and Pack 3 is the first
connection — so the free content closes on the floor moving, not on a full stop.

Packs 2, 4, 5, 7, 8, 10, 11, 13, 14 are **genuinely unconnected**. Load-bearing:
if every pack connects, players stop reading cases and start scanning for the
arc. The silence is what makes 3, 6, 9 and 12 land.

**Reveal rule:** a connection surfaces *only* in the confrontation, after the
case is already solved. Never in the briefing, never in the threads.

## Clue economy

| # | Pack | The clue |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 1 | He telephoned, never texted, about ninety seconds — and what he said was **provably false** |
| 2 | 3 | He knew something private. He had access to the victim's life long before the killer did |
| 3 | 6 | He has been doing this for decades, and he picks people already at the edge. The choosing is the crime |
| 4 | 9 | He keeps records. He follows what happens afterward — every time |
| 5 | 12 | He learned to listen professionally. Years on a crisis line, hearing people at their worst, with the training to know which ones would go |
| — | 15 | The first one was yours |

**Fair play:** every clue is a thing you can point at in the record, not an
inference the game makes for you. Clue 1 is an ordinary contradiction — the same
board, the same rules.

## Red herrings — "it could be anyone"

Once a clue is known, later packs seed suspects who match it **innocently**.

- After clue 1 (*never writes*): a suspect who never texts — because he cannot
  read well, and is ashamed of it.
- After clue 2 (*had access*): someone who knew the victim's private business
  because she cleaned the house.
- After clue 5 (*crisis line*): a suspect who volunteered on one. Thousands did.

**Rule:** a red herring must be innocent for a reason **you can also prove**. A
suspect who merely looks guilty and is then dropped is a cheat. One who looks
guilty and whose innocence you can establish with the same tools is the game
keeping its promise.

## Keeping packs different

Each pack changes the **premise**, not just the cast: a lighthouse, a night
ferry, a research station on ice, a wedding, a hospital ward, a film set, a
rowing club, a monastery guesthouse. New place, new cast, new shape of lie.

The Listener appears in 5 of 15, and speaks to you in all of them.

## Length — the honest maths

Case 1 is 76 messages and runs about 20 minutes.

**15 hours per pack is not reachable** — roughly 3,400 messages and ~50,000 words
each, fifteen times over. It also would not be fun: one mechanic sustained for
fifteen unbroken hours exhausts itself regardless of the writing.

**15 packs × ~1 hour = 15 hours.** The number you wanted, at the level where it
matters, and each pack stays inside the length where this mechanic is a pleasure.

| | |
|---|---|
| Pack | ~60 minutes, ~180–220 messages |
| Per pack | 5–6 characters, 6–7 threads, 3 contradictions + 1–2 motives |
| Campaign | **15 hours** |

**For the hackathon (2026-09-30):** Pack 1 plus the hook. Packs 2–3 if time
allows, since the free tier is the first three. Everything after is
post-submission.

## The arc hook

Three variants, each stacking different levers, per `viral-hooks`.

**A — stated contrast + you-framing** *(recommended)*
> You have never lost a case.
> He has never touched anyone.

**B — implied contrast + specificity**
> Every killer you have caught spoke to the same man first.
> None of them can tell you his name.

**C — second-order effect + reversal**
> He does not kill people. He telephones them.
> Ninety seconds, and they do it themselves.

All three: topic clear in one pass, you-framed, and a real A-vs-B gap. **A** is
the store-front line — shortest, hardest contrast, and it flatters the player
before it frightens them.

## Pack 1 — the hook

Case 1, "The Lighthouse", is already written and solvable. The hook is a
**retrofit to the confession**, not a rewrite: nothing about Mairi's guilt
changes.

Her confession already ends:

> *"I did not decide anything. I have gone over it every hour since and I cannot
> find the moment I decided."*

That line was written weeks before any of this, and it is the door. She cannot
find the moment **because it was not hers.**

**The added beat:** a man telephoned the café that evening. Said he was from the
Trust's auditors. Said Ruth had already sent the papers — that by morning it
would be out of her hands and Callum's name would be in them permanently.

**A lie, and you can prove it.** Ruth never sent anything. She wrote it in the
back of the survey notebook, in her own hand, and the notebook was still at the
scene — the epilogue already establishes this, and Esme mentions the notebook in
the group thread on day two.

**The signature, in Mairi's words:** he never once mentioned hurting Ruth. She
talked, and he listened, and when she said it out loud herself he said only:

> *"Then you already know."*

**And then the thread appears.** After the case closes, a new number in your
inbox. One message. He read about Ardnoe.

Mairi is still guilty. She still chose. But she was chosen first.
